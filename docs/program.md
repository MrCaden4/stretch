# The #1 Evidence-Based Flexibility Program for Your Resting Squat, Turnout, and Splits (2026)

The routine is the problem, not your consistency: your current 11 minutes gives almost no real time under stretch to the muscles that limit you. Over a year, being consistent with it can plausibly improve your squat a little. It cannot produce splits. The #1 program for your goals and constraints is **targeted, high-frequency static stretching of your specific limiters.** That means holds of 90 seconds to 3 minutes at 6-7/10 discomfort, loaded or contract-relax where useful, dosed at 10+ minutes per muscle group per week. On top of that, add a large volume of calf and squat-position exposure at your standing desk, and use your full-range lifting as the "strength at length" layer.

## TL;DR

- **Verdict on your current routine:** roughly 5 minutes per week per side of real calf end-range time, cut into 5-second pieces. About 5 minutes per week of hip flexor stretch, zero hamstring, and close to zero static adductor or hip-rotator stretch. Evidence thresholds start at 5-10 minutes per muscle group per week of actual stretch (Thomas et al. 2018; Ingram et al. 2024), so it is under-dosed and aimed at the wrong things for goals 2 and 3.
- **The fix:** an A/B split, 4 days a week at 22-24 minutes (Mon/Wed = squat, ankle and turnout; Tue/Thu = splits and turnout). Every muscle group gets 10+ minutes per side per week. Your StrongTek slant board becomes your main ankle tool: 30-60 minutes a day of low-intensity standing time approaches the 1-hour-a-day doses that produced actual structural change in Warneke's calf studies.
- **Honest expectations (these are estimates):** a flat-footed resting squat is realistic in about 3-6 months, and "effortless" in about 6-12. Turnout: the muscular part improves within weeks, the bony part never. Front split: likely, in 12-24 months. Side split: 18-36 months or never, depending on your hip bones. You will know which within about 12 months by the end-feel and a hard plateau.

## Key Findings

### Why years of stretching gave you almost nothing

1. **Dose.** The best meta-analyses find gains appear at about 5+ minutes per muscle per week, with daily-ish frequency. Thomas et al. 2018 (Int J Sports Med, 23 studies) concluded "at least 5 days a week for at least 5 min per week." Ingram et al. 2024 (Sports Medicine, 189 studies, 6,654 adults) found a large chronic effect (Hedges' g = 0.96), with gains maximized around 4 minutes per session and 10 minutes per week. A 2026 hamstring dose-response meta-analysis (Mendez-Rebolledo et al., Annals of Physical and Rehabilitation Medicine, 84 trials) found:
   - a single 1.5-minute dose did not exceed the 12° measurement-error threshold;
   - roughly 70 minutes accumulated over at least a week was "more likely to exceed this threshold."
   
   Most people who "stretch a lot" do 20-30 second holds of random muscles, a few days a week, and never add up the minutes.
2. **Targets.** In men, bent-knee ankle dorsiflexion (the soleus) and hip flexion were the main predictors of squat depth, together explaining 43.5% of the variance (Kim et al. 2015, Journal of Human Kinetics, 101 people). Most generic stretching goes to the hamstrings, which barely matter for a resting squat.
3. **Bone.** For side splits, and possibly your right-hip turnout, part of the limit may be skeletal. No amount of stretching fixes that. It is a reason to test, not to quit.

### What actually changes when you gain range

- **Stretch tolerance is real and counts.** Weppler and Magnusson (2010, Physical Therapy) argued that gains after 3-8 week programs come mostly from changed sensation (the "sensory theory"), not from longer muscle. The 2025 mechanisms meta-analysis by Ingram et al. (Sports Medicine) found:
   - chronic static stretching increases stretch tolerance and reduces overall stiffness;
   - both are linked to range-of-motion gains;
   - fascicle length (how long the muscle fibres are) did not change.
   
   Practical meaning: early gains are your nervous system allowing more range. That is usable range, not a trick.
- **Structural change needs big doses.** Warneke et al. 2022 (Frontiers in Physiology) stretched the calves 1 hour a day for 6 weeks with an orthosis and found real strength and muscle-thickness gains. Warneke et al. 2023 (European Journal of Applied Physiology) summarizes about 15.3% hypertrophy (d = 0.84) at 1 hour a day, 7 days a week, and strength gains of up to 22% (the 22% came from 2 hours a day and was not significantly better than 1 hour). The Warneke orthosis studies used high intensity (8/10 on a pain scale). Panidi et al. 2021 (Frontiers in Physiology; 21 adolescent female volleyball players, calf stretching 5x/week for 12 weeks) found fascicle length increases that were maintained after detraining. Nakamura's group (Frontiers in Physiology, 2021) gave 30-minute sessions twice a week for 5 weeks (3,600 s/week): dorsiflexion range and stiffness improved, but values returned to baseline after 5 weeks of detraining.
- **Conflict to know about:** Ingram 2024 found no extra benefit beyond 10 minutes per week, and no effect of intensity or frequency. Thomas 2018 found frequency matters, and the Warneke studies show structural change only at very high volume. The likely reconciliation: most trials are 4-8 week studies of ordinary range in untrained people. They do not cover splits-level range in a stiff adult. Past about 10 minutes per week, you are relying on high-volume studies (calves only) and coach experience.
- **Detraining is forgiving.** Konrad et al. 2025 (Sports Medicine - Open, 13 studies, 556 participants): stretch training produced large gains (ES 0.93). Stopping for 2-6 weeks caused a small loss (ES -0.41), but range stayed above baseline (ES 0.55). A missed week does not erase you.

### Intensity: how hard to stretch

- Ingram 2024: intensity did not moderate chronic gains.
- Acutely, Fukaya/Nakamura's group found high-intensity short stretching (120% of max tolerance, 100 s) beat low-intensity long stretching (50%, 240 s) at the same total load: +6.1° vs +3.6° dorsiflexion.
- Apostolopoulos et al. 2015 found 90% of max range (pain) caused an inflammatory response, while 30-60% did not.
- Stretch discomfort also lowers enjoyment, which kills adherence, and adherence is your known failure point.
- **Prescription:** 6-7/10 for session holds (strong, but you can breathe slowly and talk). 4-6/10 for long desk exposures. Never sharp, never a joint pinch.

### Method ranking for long-term flexibility in a lifter

| Rank | Method | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Static stretching (incl. loaded), with contract-relax (PNF) on key holds | Konrad 2024 (JSHS, 77 studies): static and PNF beat dynamic/ballistic; static vs PNF no different (p = 0.28), PNF ES -1.280 vs static -1.005. Mendez-Rebolledo 2026: PNF +3.18° over static on hamstrings (below the 12° error threshold, low certainty). Borges 2018: no significant chronic difference (-2.56°). | Core of the program |
| 2 | Resistance training through full range / at long lengths | Alizadeh 2023 (Sports Medicine): RT improves range (ES 0.73), no different from stretching. Afonso 2021 (Healthcare, 11 RCTs, n = 452): no difference (ES -0.22, 95% CI -0.55 to 0.12). Favro 2025 (JSCR): RT ES 0.73 vs control, 0.08 vs stretching. | Your lifting layer. Never shown to produce splits-level range |
| 3 | Long-duration, high-volume stretching (Warneke-style) | Only strong for calves; the only route to structural change | Used via the desk slant board |
| 4 | Loaded progressive/coach splits systems (Emmet Louis, Tom Merrick, Kurz) | Coach opinion; no trials | Borrowed for splits drills |
| 5 | FRC PAILs/RAILs, end-range isometrics | No published controlled trial in healthy adults. The only FRC trial is registered (NCT03456050, 56 low-back-pain patients, no results found). A 2025 study in the International Journal of Exercise Science (n = 28) tested CARs, not PAILs, found only acute hip internal-rotation gains, and appeared no better than static stretching. Long-length isometrics grow muscle (Oranchuk 2019), but range was not measured. | Plausible because it resembles PNF; unproven; poor range return per minute |
| 6 | Dynamic/ballistic | Konrad 2024: inferior for chronic range | Warm-up only |
| 7 | Foam rolling | Konrad 2022: chronic ES 0.82, but only after more than 4 weeks and on quads/hamstrings, not calves | Skip |

**Why the runners-up lost:** Resistance training alone matches stretching for modest range in trials, but you already lift and still can't squat flat, so more of it won't close an end-range gap. FRC has no trials and spends your minutes on 5-second contractions instead of time under stretch. Coach splits systems are good but need 30-60 minute sessions, floor space, and stretchy clothes. Pure Warneke-style dosing works but only for calves, which is exactly why the slant board carries it.

### Deep squat: what limits you, in order

1. **Ankle dorsiflexion (almost certainly your #1).** If the lowest slant board incline is brutal, you are likely below normal. Numbers:
   - For men under 50, the middle 50% on the knee-to-wall test is about 8-14 cm.
   - A 2009 Journal of Physical Therapy Science study of 71 healthy men found body weight and dorsiflexion were associated with the ability to deep squat. The cutoffs that best matched deep squatting were 10.75 cm (right) and 11.25 cm (left).
   - Target: **11-12+ cm per side.**
   - Bent-knee dorsiflexion (soleus) matters most for men (Kim 2015).
2. **Hip flexion and internal rotation.** A 2020 Journal of Physical Therapy Science motion-analysis study of 9 healthy men measured about 124-125° of hip flexion in a deep squat, with ankle dorsiflexion of about 23-26°.
3. **Center of mass and proportions.** At 6'0"-6'1" you likely have long femurs. That pushes your center of mass behind your heels at the bottom. If a kettlebell counterweight lets you sit flat but you tip backward without it, the remaining gap is a few centimeters of ankle range plus balance, not your hips.
4. **Soft-tissue approximation** (calf meeting hamstring) is the normal end point of a good squat, not a limiter.

**Training the position vs the parts:** there are no trials of "hang out in a squat for 30 minutes a day" protocols (Ido Portal's squat challenge is coach opinion only). But the squat hold loads your number-one limiter, bent-knee dorsiflexion, in the exact goal shape, and it builds minutes cheaply. Do both: component stretching for the soleus, plus accumulated squat time.

**Your "squatting makes me mobile all day" observation:** Behm et al. 2016 (Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism) found acute range gains from all stretching forms "typically lasting <30 min." The all-day feeling is probably partly perceptual, less stiffness sensation and warmth. Either way, it argues for squatting often, which the desk plan does.

### Hip turnout and your right hip

- An IADMS resource paper states that on average 60% of turnout comes from outward rotation of the hip. The rest comes from the knee, tibia (tibial torsion is bone) and foot.
- Pata et al. 2014 (Journal of Dance Medicine & Science, 6 dancers): targeted training raised Total Active Turnout by an average of 14°. That is mostly better use and control of existing range.
- Dancers used only 70-83% of their available hip external rotation in a 2020 study of 23 pre-professional dancers (Medical Problems of Performing Artists). So strength at end range (90/90 lift-offs, seated external-rotation holds) is trainable even when passive range isn't.
- **Bony factor:** increased femoral anteversion (the angle of twist in the thigh bone) restricts external rotation, while retroverted hips maximize it (Clinical Biomechanics 2022). Your 90° right-foot limit could be muscle and capsule, femoral version, or tibial torsion. The test below tells you which.

### Splits: front vs side

- **Front split is far more attainable for adult men.** It is limited by the back leg's hip flexors and hip capsule (iliofemoral ligament), and by the front leg's hamstrings. These are soft tissues that respond to training.
- **Side split** needs about 90° of abduction per hip with the pelvis tilted forward. Coaching sources summarizing a 2020 cadaveric study by Han and colleagues report bony impingement in healthy hips during high abduction combined with flexion. I could not verify the primary paper. Acetabular depth and orientation, femoral neck angle and version all vary. Some coaches (e.g., Dani Winks) argue true bony blocks are rarer than claimed. Treat it as unknown until you test it.
- **Coach protocols (coach opinion, no trials):**
  - Emmet Louis's loaded progressive stretching: about 20 reps into your "hard stop," then hold the last rep 60-90 seconds.
  - Ido Portal's version, as others describe it: 10 reps, then a 10-second hold.
  - Emmet recommends holding calf stretches at least 2 minutes per side, and up to 6 minutes for very tight calves.
  - Tom Merrick's middle-split routine (based on Emmet's work): weighted tailor pose 10 reps + 30-60 s, weighted horse stance 10 reps + 10-30 s, 3-5 sets, twice a week, adding a set weekly and deloading in week 4.
  - Emmet's front-split rule of thumb: build a long lunge with the front leg parallel to the ground first.

### Interaction with lifting and surfing

- **Stretching 10-12 hours after heavy squats blunts nothing.** Chronic stretching does not reduce strength. The Warneke studies found gains.
- **Before lifting or surfing:** Warneke & Lohmann 2024 (Journal of Sport and Health Science, 83 studies) found:
   - a small strength loss after static stretching overall (ES -0.21);
   - a large loss (ES -0.84) for holds of 60 seconds or more, but in isolated-muscle strength tests only;
   - no loss in athletic performance.
   
   A 2025 Delphi consensus of 20 experts (Warneke, Wilke et al., Journal of Sport and Health Science) advises against more than 60 seconds per muscle of static stretching right before maximal or explosive isolated efforts, and for chronic range recommends at least three sets of 120 s per muscle group per session. Before dawn patrol and lifting: dynamic warm-up and short holds only. Your 7:30pm timing is ideal.
- **Your lifting can do double duty** (Alizadeh 2023). Changes worth making, all exercises you already like or near them:
   - **Squats:** go full depth, with a 2-3 second pause in the bottom on your last set.
   - **Leg curls:** switch to seated leg curls, leaning forward, full stretch. Maeo et al. 2021 found training hamstrings at long lengths (seated) grew them more than prone curls. This is your RDL substitute.
   - **Bulgarian or ATG split squats:** deep front hip, back hip stretched under load; front-split specific.
   - **Adductor machine:** start from the widest stretch, 2-second pause (a loaded middle-split analog). Keep goblet Cossacks here as an accessory.
   - **Calf raises on a step:** 2-second pause at full stretch.
   - **Hip thrusts** stay, but they are short-length glute work and add nothing to flexibility.

## The Routine (paste into Hevy)

**General rules**
- Intensity scale: 0 = nothing, 10 = worst you'd tolerate. Holds at 6-7/10 unless noted.
- **Progression rule (all holds):** if the hold has faded to 4/10 or less by the end, go one notch deeper next session. A notch is 1 cm, one block lower, or a heavier kettlebell. Progress depth before load.
- **Contract-relax (CR), where marked:** at the listed time, push into the floor or wall for 10 s at 30-50% effort, let go, exhale, sink deeper, finish the hold.
- **Bony-pinch rule:** a sharp pinch or a hard block inside the joint means back off and change the angle. It is not a sign to push harder.
- **Hevy setup:** create each item as a custom "Duration" exercise; log kettlebell weight in the weight field. Put the setup cue in the notes.

### SESSION A: Squat / Ankle / Turnout (Monday and Wednesday, ~22 min)

**A1. Loaded Soleus Hold (half-kneeling)**
- Setup: half-kneel facing the wall, front foot flat. Kettlebell (12-24 kg) on the front thigh near the knee. Drive the knee forward over the middle toes, heel down.
- Dose: 2 sets x 90 s per side, alternating sides (the other side is your rest).
- Intensity: 7/10.
- CR: at 60 s, press the ball of the foot down for 10 s, relax and sink, then hold 20 s.
- Progress: when the stretch fades, move the foot 1 cm back from the wall; add load only after that.
- Pants: fine.

**A2. Squat Hang**
- Setup: feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out. Heels on a 1-3 cm lift (yoga block edge, book or plate) if needed. Kettlebell (8-16 kg) held at arm's length in front as a counterweight. Shift side to side, pry the knees out with the elbows.
- Dose: 4 min accumulated; break it up freely. Log the longest unbroken chunk.
- Intensity: 5-6/10.
- Progress: lower the heel lift step by step to flat, then lighten the counterweight, then remove it.
- Pants: tight at the seat and knees (see workarounds).

**A3. 90/90 Loaded Hold + Lift-Offs**
- Setup: sit in 90/90 with the front shin parallel to your chest. Hinge the chest over the front shin with a long spine (front hip external rotation). Keep the back hip down (back hip internal rotation).
- Dose: 90 s hold per side, then 5 back-leg lift-offs per side (3 s each, no hands).
- Intensity: 6-7/10.
- Progress: hands off the floor, then a light kettlebell hugged at the chest.
- Pants: restricted.

**A4. Adductor Rockback**
- Setup: on hands and knees, one leg straight out to the side, foot flat. Sit the hips back toward the heels.
- Dose: 90 s per side.
- Intensity: 6-7/10.
- CR: at 60 s, press the straight-leg foot into the floor for 10 s.
- Progress: slide the straight leg wider 1-2 cm.
- Pants: moderate.

**A5. Couch Stretch (your sequence, lengthened)**
- Setup: back knee at the wall/chair corner on the folded mat, shin up the wall, glute squeezed, ribs down.
- Dose: 1 x 2 min per side: 60 s settle, 10 s press the foot into the wall, 10 s glute squeeze, 40 s sink.
- Intensity: 7/10.
- Progress: knee closer to the wall, then torso upright, then front foot farther forward.
- Pants: fine (roll the pant leg if the knee pad binds).

### SESSION B: Splits / Turnout (Tuesday and Thursday, ~24 min)

**B1. Couch Stretch CR**
- Dose: 2 x 90 s per side: 50 s settle, 10 s press the foot into the wall, 10 s glute squeeze, 20 s sink.
- Intensity: 7/10.
- Pants: fine.

**B2. Elevated Hamstring Hold**
- Setup: heel on the seat of a chair (not rolling), leg straight, toes up. Hinge forward with a flat back, hips square. Optional kettlebell held at the chest for load.
- Dose: 2 x 90 s per side.
- Intensity: 6-7/10.
- CR: at 60 s, press the heel down into the chair for 10 s.
- Progress: move to the desk height edge, then deeper hinge, then load.
- Pants: fine.

**B3. Supported Side-Split Slide**
- Setup: face the desk, hands on it, feet wide and toes forward. Slide the feet apart (socks on carpet or sliders) until 6-7/10, pelvis slightly forward.
- Dose: 2 x 90 s.
- CR: at 30 s and 60 s, squeeze the feet toward each other for 10 s, relax, slide 1-2 cm wider.
- Progress: measure the crotch-to-floor height monthly.
- Pants: bad; needs stretch pants.

**B4. Loaded Butterfly**
- Setup: sit tall against the wall, soles together, heels 20-30 cm from the groin. Forearms press the knees down.
- Dose: 2 min.
- Intensity: 6/10.
- CR: every 30 s, lift the knees into the forearms for 5 s, then relax.
- Pants: restricted.

**B5. 90/90 Hold**
- Setup: same as A3, hold only.
- Dose: 90 s per side.
- Intensity: 6-7/10.
- Pants: restricted.

**B6. Squat Hang**
- Setup: same as A2.
- Dose: 2 min.
- Intensity: 5-6/10.
- Pants: tight at the seat and knees.

## Desk Micro-Dose Plan

This is where the ankle is won. Standing on a slant board at a standing desk is the only way a working person can approach the 1-hour-a-day calf doses of the Warneke studies.

- **Weeks 1-2:** lowest incline, one foot on the board, the other on the floor. 2 min per foot with the knee straight, then 1 min with the knee slightly bent (soleus). 4-6 bouts per shift. Intensity 4-5/10.
- **Weeks 3-6:** both feet, 3-5 min bouts, 6-10 bouts per shift, working toward 30 min per shift.
- **Week 7+:** 45-60 min a day in bouts. Raise the incline one notch when the lowest setting feels like 3/10.
- **Squat hang:** 1-2 min, counterweighted, every time you get up (bathroom, water). Aim for 4-6 a shift, heel lift allowed.
- **Hamstring:** 1 min per side, heel on a chair, once per shift.
- **What it counts for:** slant board minutes count fully toward calves. Squat hangs count toward soleus, hip flexion and partly adductors. At 30-60 min a day, desk calf volume will dwarf the session volume. That is intended.

**Honest timelines** (my estimates from the evidence plus coaching experience; there are no trials at this level):
- **(a) Resting squat:** measurable knee-to-wall gains (a change of about 1.5 cm is beyond measurement noise) within 4-8 weeks. Flat-footed and unsupported in about 3-6 months; effortless in about 6-12.
- **(b) Turnout:** active external-rotation control improves in 6-12 weeks (Pata 2014 saw +14° active turnout). Passive range gains are smaller and slower, and the bony share never changes.
- **(c) Front split:** 12-24 months at about 90 min a week, with steady, visible block-count drops.
- **(d) Side split:** 18-36 months, or anatomically off the table.

**How to tell bone from muscle:**
- **Muscular / soft tissue:** the stretch is felt in the belly of the muscle (inner thigh, buttock). The end-point is springy and shifts with warm-up, time of day and contract-relax. Numbers creep over months.
- **Bony:**
  - a hard, abrupt stop at the exact same point every day, unchanged by warm-up or contract-relax;
  - a pinch at the front or side of the hip joint rather than a stretch in the muscle;
  - no side-split change over 12+ months of consistent work while other tests improve.
- **Right-hip turnout check:** lie face down with the knees bent to 90° and measure hip internal and external rotation on both sides with the phone.
  - If the right hip has much more internal rotation and less external rotation, but the total arc is similar to the left, suspect femoral anteversion (bone). Train active control, and don't force passive external rotation.
  - If the right total arc is smaller and the end-feel is soft, it is soft tissue and trainable.
  - Also check tibial torsion: sit with the knee bent, and see which way the foot points relative to the kneecap. That part is bone.

**Lifelong maintenance:** once you reach a goal, drop to 3 sessions a week of 15 minutes. The detraining data (Konrad 2025) suggest gains fade slowly but do fade (Nakamura 2021), so never go to zero.

## Caveats

- Hold times above 2 minutes, splits timelines, the squat-accumulation idea, and all coach protocols rest on coach opinion or indirect evidence. The meta-analyses mostly cover modest range over 4-12 weeks.
- The knee-to-wall squat cutoffs come from one study in Japanese men. Your proportions may shift your number.
- The side-split cadaver study was only available secondhand.
- There is no chronic-range evidence for PAILs/RAILs.
- If you get hip pain with pinching, clicking or catching, stop that position and see a sports physio. A pinch in forced flexion with internal rotation can signal impingement, and more stretching will not fix it.
- Optional home add-on: a 3-minute squat hang while the coffee brews on Fri-Sun. The program does not depend on it. Surfing and skating are your "non-vegetable" days.
